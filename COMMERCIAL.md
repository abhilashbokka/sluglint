# Commercial licensing

Sluglint is released under the [PolyForm Noncommercial License 1.0.0](LICENSE).
Read the licence itself for the binding terms. This page is a plain summary.

## Free, no permission needed

- Writing your own screenplays, whether or not you hope to sell them
- Students, teachers, and academic research
- Charities, non-profits, public research bodies, government institutions
- Hobby projects, experiments, blog posts, conference talks
- Forking it, changing it, and sharing your changes, as long as the use stays
  noncommercial and the licence notice travels with the copy

Writing a spec script you hope a studio buys is a personal use. Sluglint costs
you nothing.

## Needs a commercial licence

- A production company, studio, or agency running it on scripts as part of its
  work, even entirely internally
- Bundling it into a product you sell, or a service you charge for
- Running it as a hosted service for other people
- Consultancies and script-coverage businesses using it on client scripts

The test is the purpose of the use, not whether money changes hands in that
specific moment. A studio linting its own slate internally is commercial use.

## Getting a commercial licence

Open an issue titled `Commercial licence enquiry`, or email the address on
[github.com/abhilashbokka](https://github.com/abhilashbokka). Tell me roughly
what you want to do with it and how big the team is, and I will send terms.

Current shapes, all negotiable while the project is young:

| Shape | Who it fits |
|---|---|
| Single production | One film or one season, one licence |
| Team seat | A writers' room or development slate, billed per year |
| OEM / embedded | You want the engine inside a product you sell |
| Sponsored rule work | You need a profile or rule set Sluglint does not have yet |

## Credit

The licence requires that the `Required Notice:` line from [LICENSE](LICENSE)
travels with any copy you pass on. That is the only obligation.

Beyond that, a mention is appreciated and never required. If Sluglint caught
something useful in your draft, saying so publicly helps more than money does at
this stage.

## Why this licence

The rulebook, the engine, and every check are readable by anyone. Since the
whole claim of the project is that its findings are checkable, hiding the rules
would undercut the only thing that separates it from a chatbot with a prompt.

What the licence stops is a company taking the work, selling it, and returning
nothing. Individual writers, the people it was built for, pay nothing and always
will.
